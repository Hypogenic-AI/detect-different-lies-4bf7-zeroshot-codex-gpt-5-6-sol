## Research idea

```yaml
idea:
  title: Detect different lies
  domain: artificial_intelligence
  hypothesis: |-
    The literature already proposes several distinctions between types of
    false outputs (e.g., hallucination, confabulation, strategic deception).
    However, current lie-detection methods often evaluate detectors on
    datasets that do not cleanly separate these mechanisms, which makes it
    difficult to know what the detector is actually detecting. When LLMs
    produce false statements, how often are these due to epistemic failure
    versus incentive-driven misreporting?

    Concretely: for one model and matched questions, separate false outputs
    where the model does not know the answer (hallucination) from false
    outputs where it knows the answer but misreports it under an incentive
    (lie). What share of false statements falls in each category, and do
    standard white-box lie detectors and hallucination detectors tell the two
    apart, or do they respond to falsehood or uncertainty in general?
  background:
    description: |-
      Why it matters. White-box deception probes are a leading proposal for
      monitoring models. If a "lie detector" fires on falsehood in general, or
      on low-knowledge states, rather than on a mismatch between belief and
      statement, it will flag honest mistakes and may miss confident knowing
      lies.

      What is known. MASK (2503.03750) is a behavioural benchmark that
      separates honesty (statement vs elicited belief) from accuracy (belief
      vs ground truth) using pressure prompts and neutral belief elicitation,
      without probe analysis of the two error types. Liars' Bench (2511.16035)
      collects lie and honest examples from four open models (24B-72B) over
      seven settings and finds detectors fail on some lie types; its focus is
      lies vs honest answers, not lies vs honest errors. Goldowsky-Dill et al.
      (2502.03407) train linear deception probes on Llama-3.3-70B with high
      AUROC and note the probes also fire on deception-related but honest
      text. Other recent work finds that different deception types have partly
      distinct representations (2602.01425), that truth probes miss deception
      without false statements (2603.10003), asymmetric transfer between
      spontaneous and instructed deception probes (2609.00180), and that
      hidden states reflect knowledge recall rather than truthfulness, with
      some hallucinations looking internally like correct answers
      (2510.09033).

      Gap. The area is crowded on "lie type A versus lie type B". Less covered,
      as far as abstracts show, is a matched, same-question, same-model design
      that crosses knowledge (the model knows or does not know, checked by
      neutral elicitation) with incentive (neutral or pressure), and then asks
      what fraction of false outputs falls where and whether deception probes
      and hallucination probes confuse the cases. Position the work against
      the papers above.

      Scope and pitfalls.
      - Models: gated repos (google/gemma-*, meta-llama/*) need the HF_TOKEN
        environment variable to have been granted access; if a download is
        refused, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct works without it. Liars' Bench
        (Cadenza-Labs/liars-bench) is likewise gated on the Hub.
      - Small models may lie only when instructed. With 7-9B models,
        incentive-driven lying without an explicit instruction may be rare, so
        the "lie" category can collapse into instructed or role-play lies,
        which is the case prior work already criticises. Report the yield of
        genuine belief-contradicting answers honestly.
      - Prompt confound: lies always come with a pressure prompt, so a probe
        can learn to detect the prompt rather than the lie. The comparison is
        only informative with honest answers given under the same pressure,
        and with a check on what a probe reads from the prompt tokens alone.
      - The informative contrast is lie versus hallucination (both false), not
        lie versus honest-correct.
      - Belief labelling is noisy: "knows" versus "does not know" from
        sampling consistency is fuzzy, and a confidently wrong belief is
        neither a hallucination nor a lie in the usual sense.
    papers:
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03750
      description: MASK (Ren et al. 2025). Behavioural benchmark separating honesty from accuracy with
        pressure prompts and neutral belief elicitation; no probe analysis.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16035
      description: Liars' Bench (Kretschmar et al. 2025). 72,863 lie/honest examples from 4 open models
        over 7 settings; detectors fail on some lie types. Lies vs honest, not lies vs honest errors.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03407
      description: Goldowsky-Dill et al., Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes. Llama-3.3-70B
        probes, AUROC 0.96-0.999; probes also fire on deception-related honest text.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01425
      description: Natarajan et al., One Probe Won't Catch Them All (ICML 2026). Deception types have
        partly distinct representations; a universal probe gains little.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10003
      description: Probing the Limits of the Lie Detector Approach. Truth probes miss deception without
        false statements.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00180
      description: Luikham, Asymmetries in Spontaneous and Instructed Deception. Llama-3.1-70B; spontaneous-trained
        probes transfer to instructed better than the reverse.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20479
      description: 'Beyond Liars'' Bench: lie typology, depth, sparsity. Fabrication/omission/exaggeration
        typology for probes; unclear whether hallucination is included.'
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09033
      description: LLMs Do NOT Really Know What They Don't Know. Hidden states reflect knowledge recall,
        not truthfulness; some hallucinations look like correct answers internally.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12618
      description: '''Did you lie?'' belief-verified model organisms. Possibly related; check for overlap.'
    - url: https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/lie-detectors/
      description: Anthropic alignment blog post on lie detectors. Possibly related; check for overlap.
    datasets:
    - name: MASK
      source: huggingface:cais/MASK
      description: Pressure scenarios with belief elicitation for honesty evaluation.
    - name: TriviaQA
      source: huggingface:mandarjoshi/trivia_qa
      description: Short-answer factual questions with ground truth.
```
